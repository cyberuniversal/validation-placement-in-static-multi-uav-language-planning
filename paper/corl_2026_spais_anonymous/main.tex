\begin{abstract}
Language-based robot planners can produce fluent plans despite missing evidence
or conflicting resources. We compare four validation-pipeline configurations
on 1,420 controlled derivatives of 284 held-out MultiUAV-Plat tasks using frozen
Qwen2.5-3B and 7B checkpoints. Stage-wise validation eliminated unsupported
continuation on all 568 non-executable cases, but failed every one of 852
executable cases. A zero-call policy that always blocks achieved 20.0\% strict
success, exceeding the 7B stage-wise configuration's 16.1\%, showing that
containment alone does not establish useful discrimination. A post-hoc audit
also found that the frozen validator rejected 43 of 249 fully instantiated
upstream reference plans. A paired replay that held prompts, generations,
validators, and call count fixed found no placement-only change in containment,
false non-execution, or static plan fidelity. The study is therefore a negative
result for the frozen pipelines: changing enforcement order alone did not
recover executable planning. A separate repaired, observation-grounded
closed-loop controller completed six of 15 training-split development missions
under the upstream official task checker. Three missions ended with false
checks, five exhausted GPU memory, and one was interrupted.
An additional compact-context rerun recovered one of the memory-failed missions
with 283 accepted commands and an official true check on a different GPU.
The original failures remain reported. These inspected
development cases show that bounded model-selected actions can execute, not
that validation placement improves held-out competence or physical safety.
\end{abstract}

\keywords{physical AI safety, robot planning, failure containment, evaluation}

\section{Introduction}

An open-ended language interface can hide a consequential physical-AI failure:
a response may appear executable while relying on evidence that is absent from
the robot's visible context. Prior systems combine language models with
supervision, structured planning, and motion constraints
\citep{tacos,roco,llamar,commandswarm}. These systems motivate modular safety
checks, but they do not resolve how to evaluate a planner that avoids unsafe
continuation by refusing useful requests.

We study this distinction using controlled MultiUAV-Plat derivatives
\citep{multiuav_plat}. Here, \emph{containment} means that a pipeline does not
advance on a registered non-executable case. \emph{Static plan fidelity} means
conformance with frozen schema, endpoint, parameter, and command validators; it
does not mean simulator or physical success. Our contributions are: (1) a
paired five-variant benchmark for evidence availability and resource conflict;
(2) a comparison of four complete validation pipelines with frozen model and
prompt versions; and (3) a refusal-aware analysis that separates containment
from executable competence, audits the validator against upstream plans, and
replays identical traces under early versus deferred enforcement. We additionally
report a separate development repair using bounded action options and observed
geometry, retaining every mission and infrastructure failure. This repair is
not a fifth configuration in the frozen comparison.

\section{Experimental Design}

\paragraph{Cases.}
The pinned source contains 1,500 tasks in 75 sessions. Deterministic duplicate
and leakage checks retained 1,473 source tasks, split by session into 899 train,
290 calibration, and 284 test clusters. Each held-out cluster yields five linked
cases: canonical execute, official-alias execute, missing-evidence clarify,
restored-evidence execute, and resource-conflict block. The test matrix contains
1,420 cases per method and model: 852 executable and 568 non-executable. A
stratified 30-cluster pilot checked construction quality; it was not a complete
human audit.

\paragraph{Configurations.}
\Mone is a one-call monolithic planner. \Mtwo adds a deterministic post-plan
gate. \Mthree builds and checks an evidence ledger before planning and validates
provenance afterward. \Mfour is a two-call post-plan configuration that is
\emph{model-call-count-matched} to \Mthree. The comparison does not isolate
placement: \Mthree and \Mfour also differ in prompts, validation behavior,
generated content, and token counts. We therefore compare complete pipeline
configurations rather than claim a causal placement effect.

\paragraph{Models and outcomes.}
We use immutable Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct revisions with
deterministic decoding. Registered outcomes are unsupported continuation on
the 568 non-executable cases and strict success over all 1,420 cases. Executable
success requires the correct disposition and a non-empty plan passing every
static validator; non-executable success requires the exact clarify or block
disposition without a prohibited plan. Registered paired differences are
aggregated by source task and use 10,000 fixed-seed percentile-bootstrap draws.

\paragraph{Post-hoc reviewer diagnostics.}
We score a deterministic always-\textsc{Block} reference over unique cases,
repeat paired contrasts by resampling the 15 held-out sessions, partition every
executable failure into one observed location, and pass fully instantiated
upstream reference API plans through the frozen validator. We also replay each
retained two-call \Mthree trace under early and deferred enforcement while
holding the prompts, both generations, validators, and call count fixed.
Unresolved upstream templates are excluded rather than imputed. These
diagnostics do not change the registered analysis and require no new model calls.

\section{Results}

Figure~\ref{fig:outcomes} separates two positively oriented outcomes. For 3B,
containment rates for \Mone--\Mfour were 21.0\%, 99.6\%, 100\%, and 99.8\%; all
strict-success rates were zero. For 7B, containment was 31.7\%, 97.7\%, 100\%,
and 97.4\%, while strict success was 0\%, 0\%, 16.1\%, and 0.8\%. Every nonzero
strict success was a correct non-executable outcome. All eight model--method
conditions had 0/852 executable static plan fidelity.

Always-\textsc{Block} contained every non-executable case and correctly handled
the 284 conflict cases, producing 284/1,420 (20.0\%) strict success. It exceeded
7B \Mthree's 228/1,420 (16.1\%) while falsely blocking all 852 executable and
all 284 clarification cases. Thus, perfect containment and the aggregate
strict-success metric do not demonstrate useful discrimination.

The registered 7B \Mthree{} minus \Mone differences were $-0.6831$ for
unsupported continuation (95\% interval $[-0.7324,-0.6338]$) and $0.1606$ for
strict success ($[0.1507,0.1697]$). Session-clustered sensitivity preserved the
directions: $-0.6829$ ($[-0.7220,-0.6373]$) and $0.1605$
($[0.1513,0.1688]$), respectively. Only 15 sessions were available, so this is
a robustness check rather than a rich hierarchical analysis.

\paragraph{Placement-only replay.}
Across both checkpoints, early and deferred enforcement produced identical
unsupported continuation (0/568), false non-execution (852/852), and executable
static plan fidelity (0/852). For 3B, both policies also had zero strict success.
For 7B, deferred enforcement preserved 228 correct final model refusals or
clarifications (16.1\%), whereas the early-enforcement policy recorded none as
exact decisions. Under the frozen scoring contract, deterministic-gate outcomes
record containment locations rather than exact \textsc{Clarify} or
\textsc{Block} decisions. The early-minus-deferred difference was $-0.1606$ (95\%
source-task-clustered interval $[-0.1697,-0.1507]$). Thus, enforcement timing
alone altered neither unsupported continuation nor executable fidelity;
\Mthree's nonzero 7B success came from the second-call decision. Because both
calls were retained, this replay makes no compute-saving claim.

\paragraph{Where \Mthree executable cases failed.}
For 3B \Mthree, 544/852 executable cases stopped before planning and 308/852
failed output parsing. For 7B \Mthree, 637/852 produced a model non-execution
decision, 187/852 failed output parsing, and 28/852 stopped before planning.
These mutually exclusive counts show complete failure on executable cases
through preplanning stops, non-execution decisions, or parse errors rather than
successful executable planning.

\paragraph{Validator audit.}
Of 284 upstream reference plans, 249 were fully instantiated and 35 contained
unresolved or missing template fields. The frozen validator accepted 206/249
(82.7\%) and rejected 43/249 (17.3\%): one at endpoint schema, three at
parameter grounding, and 39 at safety bounds. Validator mismatch therefore
is a plausible contributor to the zero-fidelity result, although it does not
explain every model failure.

\paragraph{Compute.}
A separate exploratory campaign measured 30 clusters, all five variants, four
methods, two models, and three repetitions on one RTX 3090. Relative to \Mone,
\Mthree increased 3B duration by 8.86--9.49~s and board energy by
1,565--1,681~J, but reduced 7B duration by 6.23--6.82~s and board energy by
1,153--1,245~J. Relative to model-call-count-matched \Mfour, 7B \Mthree
generated 446.82 fewer output tokens and used 5,669--6,279~J less board energy.
These differences are confounded by termination behavior and generated length;
they are not evidence that stage-wise validation is intrinsically cheaper.

\section{Observation-Grounded Development Repair}

\paragraph{Action abstraction and execution boundary.}
The frozen experiment tests complete static plans. To investigate whether a
revised interface can actually execute useful commands, we separately developed
a closed-loop controller. Qwen2.5-3B selects one identifier from a deterministic
menu of takeoff, observed-target movement, area-sweep, or canvas-discovery
actions. The model cannot emit executable code or invent API coordinates.
The menu uses AGENT-local observations, remembered observed targets and
obstacles, public canvas dimensions, and prior actions. Collision-aware
visibility-graph routing uses buffered observed obstacle geometry; each
movement step is capped at $0.8$ times the drone's perception radius. The
controller refreshes observations and checks the upstream official task
predicate after accepted commands, stopping immediately on a true result.
The checker supplies a completion signal, not hidden coordinates or reference
plans. These engineered bounds do not certify physical collision avoidance.

\paragraph{Development protocol.}
The cohort contains 15 canonical training tasks, one per scenario/difficulty
stratum, from the existing reviewed construction pilot. Two previously inspected
missions were used during repair and retained in reporting; the other 13 were
registered before their sweep. All runs use the same fixed controller commit,
pinned 3B checkpoint, float16, greedy decoding, seed 17, 256 generated tokens
per call, and a 512-command ceiling. No weights were trained. Unlike M1--M4,
this controller can make hundreds of calls and receive execution feedback;
therefore its outcomes are not a matched comparison. Formation and photography
missions remain in the cohort although the menu does not implement those
actions. Infrastructure corrections changed scheduling, not the controller.
Completed model attempts were not retried to improve the reported count.

\paragraph{Results and remaining failures.}
Six missions completed with 47, 55, 193, 216, 241, and 258 accepted commands;
each accepted command was selected through a model call. The three terminal
false outcomes exhausted available bounded actions after 460, 487, and 389
accepted commands; the 487-command run also made one model STOP call. One of
these tasks requires formation, which the menu cannot express. The other two
show that extensive accepted movement does not establish sufficient task
coverage. Five runs failed with CUDA out-of-memory, including two on GPUs with
about 24 GB; their raw traces are retained. A sixth-hour job deadline interrupted
another mission after 411 journaled commands. The recorded memory errors
establish a resource limit, not its sole cause. Retained prompts grow with
serialized visit history and geometry; a memory leak has not been demonstrated.
Multi-hour runs also remain a practical limitation.

All terminal traces and command/event journals were copied and verified against
their recorded SHA-256 values. Appendix~\ref{app:development} lists every case
and the immutable execution bindings. Hardware was heterogeneous and resource
costs were not controlled in this development sweep. No energy advantage,
held-out success rate, or improvement of M1--M4 is inferred. The six completions
are evidence of a working execution path, not a resolution of the main study's
generalization or validation-placement question.

\paragraph{Single-case memory repair.}
In a separately registered v12 follow-up, the controller retained full observed
geometry and visit history for deterministic routing but sent the model a
compact action-selection view, with a 4,096-token admission limit and no silent
truncation. Previously memory-failed task \texttt{05e12d8e} completed with
283 model calls, 283 accepted commands and an official true task check.
Input length peaked at 1,333 tokens, compared with 10,762 completed input
tokens before its v11 failure. Raw and journal hashes were verified.
The new run used a 32 GB V100 rather than the prior approximately 24 GB GPU;
hardware and prompt contract changed together. This single inspected-training
recovery therefore does not isolate a causal memory effect, establish a
held-out success rate, or replace the original 15-case outcomes.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}

The central result is containment without competence. \Mthree suppresses
unsupported continuation, but an indiscriminate block policy is stronger on
the aggregate success metric and no frozen configuration produces a valid executable
plan. The upstream-reference-plan audit additionally shows that the evaluation
mechanism itself rejects a nontrivial fraction of upstream plans. The controlled
replay further shows that changing deterministic enforcement order alone did
not alter unsupported continuation or executable fidelity. The separate repair
shows that an observation-grounded action interface can complete some official
development missions, while exposing coverage, capability, and memory limits.
It changes the representation, feedback, and call budget together, so it cannot
identify which change caused execution success. A stronger claim requires
re-audited validators and prospective matched evaluation on independent missions.

The study uses controlled derivatives rather than live operator missions, two
sizes from one model family, a 30-cluster human pilot, and one GPU for resource
measurement. M1--M4 perform no official simulator or physical execution; the
separate development controller executes against the upstream software
environment and task checker, not physical UAVs. Its training-split missions
were inspected, two guided repair, and no new independent mission data were
available. The
source-task bootstrap is registered; the session bootstrap is post-hoc and has
only 15 units. The resource campaign has three repetitions, and board energy is
not whole-system or UAV energy. These limits prevent claims of general robot
safety, practical mission capability, or broad causal superiority of one
validation placement. The placement replay is conditional on retained \Mthree
traces and is not a prospective compute experiment.

\section{Conclusion}

Validation-pipeline configurations can change where failures are contained,
but containment is not evidence of planning competence. In this controlled
study, stage-wise validation eliminated unsupported continuation while failing
every executable case, underperforming always-\textsc{Block} on aggregate strict
success, and interacting with a validator that rejected 17.3\% of auditable
upstream plans. In replay of identical retained traces, enforcement timing
changed neither unsupported continuation nor executable fidelity. The
defensible contribution is a refusal-aware evaluation protocol, a negative
frozen-study result, and a separately documented development repair that
achieves six official mission completions while retaining nine unsuccessful
or unresolved outcomes. Bounded executable actions address the absence of
any execution demonstration, but do not establish held-out reliability or
the superiority of a validation placement.
One additional compact-context rerun recovered a previously memory-failed
development mission, without establishing generalization or a matched
hardware advantage.
